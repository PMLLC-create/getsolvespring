Percentages show up everywhere: sales, tips, raises, test scores, interest rates. Nearly every percentage question is one of four types. Figure out which one you have, and the math is one step.

## Percent means "per hundred"

25% means 25 out of every 100, or 0.25 as a decimal. To turn a percent into a decimal, divide by 100 (move the decimal point two places left). 7% is 0.07, 150% is 1.5.

## The four questions

### 1. What is X% of a number?

<p class="formula">Answer = (X ÷ 100) × number</p>

*15% of $80:* 0.15 × 80 = **$12**.

### 2. X is what percent of Y?

<p class="formula">Percent = (X ÷ Y) × 100</p>

*You answered 42 out of 50 questions correctly:* 42 ÷ 50 × 100 = **84%**.

### 3. What is the percent change?

<p class="formula">Percent change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100</p>

*Rent went from $1,250 to $1,350:* (1,350 − 1,250) ÷ 1,250 × 100 = **8% increase**.

*A stock fell from $40 to $34:* (34 − 40) ÷ 40 × 100 = **−15%**, a 15% decrease.

### 4. Increase or decrease a number by X%

<p class="formula">Increase: number × (1 + X ÷ 100)<br>Decrease: number × (1 − X ÷ 100)</p>

*A $50,000 salary with a 4% raise:* 50,000 × 1.04 = **$52,000**.

*A $90 item at 30% off:* 90 × 0.70 = **$63**.

## Working backward: finding the original number

If you know the result after a percent change, divide instead of multiply.

*A shirt is $42 after 30% off. What was the original price?* $42 ÷ 0.70 = **$60**.

*Your paycheck is $2,080 after a 4% raise. What was it before?* $2,080 ÷ 1.04 = **$2,000**.

A common mistake is to add the percent back: $42 + 30% = $54.60. That's wrong because the 30% was taken from the larger original price.

## Mental math shortcuts

- **10%:** move the decimal one place left. 10% of $64 is $6.40.
- **5%:** half of 10%. 5% of $64 is $3.20.
- **20%:** double 10%. 20% of $64 is $12.80.
- **15%:** 10% plus half of that. $6.40 + $3.20 = $9.60.
- **1%:** move the decimal two places. 1% of $2,350 is $23.50.
- **Swap trick:** X% of Y equals Y% of X. 8% of 50 is the same as 50% of 8, which is 4.

## Percent vs. percentage points

When a rate changes, there are two ways to describe it. If an interest rate goes from 4% to 5%:

- It rose by **1 percentage point** (5 − 4).
- It rose by **25 percent** ((5 − 4) ÷ 4 × 100).

Both are correct, but they mean different things. News stories and loan offers often use whichever sounds more dramatic, so check which one you're reading.

## Percent increases and decreases aren't symmetric

If something drops 50% and then rises 50%, you don't end up where you started. $100 down 50% is $50. $50 up 50% is $75. To get back to $100 from $50, you need a 100% increase. Keep this in mind with investments, prices, and hours cut and restored.
